# Ezypick — My Narration Script (~2 min)

*Trimmed for a 2-minute video. Times are rough targets — aim to finish under 2:00.*

---

**Slide 1 — Cover** *(~5s)*
> Hi — this is Ezypick.

**Slide 2 — Big Idea (Decision Fatigue)** *(~8s)*
> A good meal can make your day — but every decision we make drains a little of our limited energy.

**Slide 3 — Essential Question** *(~7s)*
> That led to my essential question: how do we make deciding where to eat easier?

**Slide 4 — The Challenge** *(~10s)*
> So my challenge: take the effort out of choosing where to eat. Simple to say — but the real difficulty was hidden underneath.

**Slide 5 — Domain Investigation** *(~9s)*
> To dig in, I asked friends how they really pick a place — the most frustrating part, and what finally makes them commit.

**Slide 6 — Meet Alex** *(~11s)*
> Take Alex — 23, works in the city, surrounded by restaurants. Alone, he defaults to the same takeaway; with colleagues, everyone just pushes "you decide" around.

**Slide 7 — Problem & Opportunity** *(~16s)*
> Here's the real problem: one simple question hides a dozen factors — weather, distance, budget, who's coming. Alone we settle; in a group no one commits. Today's apps help you browse, not decide. The opportunity: turn it into one confident choice.

**Slide 8 — Solution Ideas** *(~12s)*
> So I brainstormed a few approaches: smart filters, a random "surprise me", a map like today, or swiping yes-or-no on cuisine and vibe — all aiming at an easier decision.

**Slide 9 — Decision Matrix** *(~10s)*
> To pick one, I scored each on effort, speed, and group use. Swiping won — it's low-effort, fast, and still works with others.

**Slide 10 — From Idea to Prototype** *(~12s)*
> Then I planned the build: for each key question — what to ask, where the data comes from, how group swipes combine — I paired an activity, up to a clickable prototype.

**Slide 11 — The App: Ezypick** *(~13s)*
> And this is Ezypick. A few swipes on cuisine and vibe, and it picks one spot nearby — solo, or matched across your group. No scrolling, no ordering. These are the first rough screens.

**Slide 12 — Close** *(~5s)*
> Thanks for watching — that's Ezypick: one gesture, one decision.
